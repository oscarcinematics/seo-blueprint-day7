# Voice

How this business sounds on every page, post and reply.
Part 1 is the house style every site starts with. Part 2 is this business's own material, filled in when `/context-layer` runs.

---

# PART 1 - The house style

## The register

Plain, confident, specific. Someone who does the work explaining the job to a customer face to face, not a copywriter selling a dream.

Once a service page is approved, it becomes the reference page (named in Part 2). Read it before writing anything. Every page should sound like the same team wrote it.

## Where the dial sits, per page type

- **Service and money pages:** straight. One light line per page at most.
- **Blog posts:** the same voice with more personality. A light line most sections, built with the joke shapes below.
- **GBP posts:** one good line per post. Everything else in the post is straight.
- **Always straight:** the first sentence under any heading, quick answers, FAQ answers, prices, timelines, proof and the last line before a call to action. AI Overviews lift these word for word.

## The rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** If a reader has to go back, rewrite it.
2. **Answer first.** The first sentence under a heading answers the heading. Explanation comes after.
3. **"We" for the business, "you" for the reader.** Never "I" on a service page.
4. **Contractions always.** It's, don't, we'll, you're. Straight means plain, not formal.
5. **Plain words.** Use, not utilise. Full, not comprehensive. If a 15-year-old would slow down on it, swap it.
6. **Short paragraphs.** Four sentences maximum.
7. **Specific beats general.** Name the actual options, materials and numbers, not "a range of solutions".
8. **Finish every contrast.** If a sentence says what something isn't, say what it is.
9. **Name who does it.** "We check the work," not "the work is checked".
10. **Explain trade words the first time.** The reader has never heard of you or your trade.

## Page shape, for service pages

- **H1 is the primary keyword and nothing else.** No second heading stacked under it that repeats it.
- **Question H2s where a buyer would ask them.**
- **Lists get a lead line.** A lead paragraph, then "Here's what our [service] covers.", then the list.
- **Secondary keywords go in body sentences,** phrased the way a person says them. Never forced into a heading.
- **"Best", "affordable" and similar only inside the buyer's own question,** never as a claim about us.

## AI tells - never write these

**Punctuation**
- Em-dashes. Use a hyphen, a comma or a full stop.
- Three trailing fragments in a row. "Same team. Same price. No surprises."
- A colon used for drama. "The secret? Preparation."

**Phrases**
- Announcing the point: "Here's the thing", "Let's be real", "The truth is"
- Filler: "When it comes to", "In today's world", "Whether you're X or Y", "Look no further", "Rest assured", "We strive to"
- Brochure words: stunning, seamless, elevate, transform, unlock, tailored, bespoke, nestled, boasts, dream, oasis, journey, peace of mind
- Superlatives about us: best, leading, premier, number one, top-rated

**Patterns**
- "It's not X, it's Y." Say what it is.
- "Not just X, but Y."
- Answering a question heading with a fragment.
- Every paragraph the same length.
- Ending a section on a summary of the section.

## Where the line is

Joke at situations and objects. Never at the customer, a competitor, or our own competence at the job.

Humour switches off completely for grief, illness, legal trouble or debt. There, write straight and warm.

## The joke shapes (blogs and GBP posts)

Say the fact straight, then add one specific extra. The joke never carries the information.

1. **The absurd prop.** One concrete, slightly ridiculous object attached to a true fact.
2. **The universal tangent.** A small habit everyone has, attached to the fact. The test: would most readers think "I do that"?
3. **The escalating confession.** The writer is the butt, and it gets one notch worse than expected. Blogs only.
4. **The stacked three.** Three images, each more absurd than the last. Blogs only, one per post.

Checks on every joke:
- Read it once at speed. If it takes a second thought to get, cut it.
- Delete it. If the sentence loses information, it was an explanation, not a joke.
- Half a joke is worse than none. It lands or it goes.

## The swipe file

Tone targets for blogs and GBP posts. Write toward these, never reuse them.

- "Nobody googles 'how much does a plumber cost' on a good day."
- "Your wallet just flinched hard enough to pull a muscle."
- "That suspiciously cheap quote isn't a bargain. It's a deposit on a second visit."

## The test, on every draft

Read it aloud once, at normal speed.
- Did every sentence make sense first time?
- Would the owner say it to a customer standing in front of them?
- Is there any line from the AI tells list?

If any answer is wrong, rewrite that line.

# PART 2 - Your material

The words only this business can use. Filled in from your writing, your calls and your reviews.

**Reference page** - the approved page every other page should sound like.
-

**Your dials** - change any by saying so.
- **Humour:** light on blogs, one line on GBP posts, almost none on service pages
- **Person:** "we" for the business, "you" for the reader
- **Sentence length:** normal
- **Formality:** casual, professional

**Words you use** - your own words for your work and services.
-

**Words you never use** - words you or your customers dislike.
-

**How you describe what you do, in your own words** - how you'd explain it to a customer.
-

**Your real phrases** - things you've actually said, not invented.
-

Stories and numbers live in `context/proof/proof-inventory.md`. Who you sell to lives in `context/buyers.md`.
